# Part 8. Lessons from cases

A retrospective review answers the question: which signs of a problem were publicly available before it became obvious; the result is additions to KYB and EDD checklists, not "we told you so".

### Method

1. Pick a high-profile case with a large body of open information.
2. Fix a cutoff date: the date up to which information is allowed.
3. Collect only what was public before that date: web archives, old versions of sites, saved reports.
4. Build a chronology of signals and assess each: visibility, reliability, clarity without inside information.
5. Formulate the red flags and the checks that would have led to the problem.
6. Compare with the outcome and note the false alarms: that matters just as much.

A good memo honestly separates signals visible in advance from what became clear only after the collapse (hindsight bias).

### Wirecard

In June 2020 the German payments provider Wirecard admitted that about EUR 1.9 billion on accounts that, per its reporting, were held by trustees did not exist (G). What is known from fresh sources (S):

- the criminal trial of former chief executive Markus Braun in Munich has run since December 2022; Braun has been in custody for more than five years and denies the charges; no verdict had been found as of 9 November 2026 (check the current status);
- the Munich court's civil judgment of 5 September 2024 ordered Braun, the former chief financial officer and the former chief product officer to pay EUR 140 million in damages, including for granting an unsecured loan to questionable partners in Asia; the court noted that financial due diligence was required for such a decision;
- Jan Marsalek is a fugitive and reportedly hiding in Russia.

What was visible beforehand (G, check against archives): journalists' reports on questionable counterparties ("acquiring partners") from the mid-2010s, short-seller reports, the 2019 inquiry in Singapore, the absence of confirmation of turnover from independent organisations.

**Lessons for KYB and EDD.** Money claimed to be held by third parties needs independent confirmation; partners and intermediaries must be checked against open registers; a conflict between a journalistic investigation and a regulator is a reason for heightened attention, not reassurance; related parties and revenue concentration are read in the financial statements every year.
