# Part 5. EDD

## 5.1 Triggers, content, approval and monitoring

EDD is launched by a risk trigger and ends with a management decision with recorded grounds; enhanced checks without a decision turn into collecting documents.

### Triggers (G: the list is set by your policy and the law)

| Trigger | Example |
| --- | --- |
| PEPs, family members, close associates | A member of parliament, an official, their relatives |
| High-risk country | FATF black and grey lists, European Commission lists, sanctions |
| Complex structure | Multi-layer ownership, trusts, nominees, offshore |
| Unusual transactions | Atypical volumes, payments to third parties, rapid changes |
| Adverse media | Investigations, claims, sanctions links |
| Large sums or wealth | For wealth management and fortunes of EUR 50 million or more (per the AMLR, S) |
| Crypto-assets | A source of funds in virtual assets |
| Refusal or evasion | No documents, contradictory explanations |

### Content of EDD

1. **Additional identification** of the customer, beneficial owner and controllers (section 2.2).
2. **Source of wealth and source of funds** with documents and independent checks (section 2.8).
3. **A deeper check of ownership and control** down to individuals, with shares calculated under several regimes (section 3.2).
4. **Screening and adverse media** for all related persons and companies, with re-screening (sections 2.6, 4.1, 4.2).
5. **Checking the activity:** website, staff, contracts, assets, financial statements (section 3.3).
6. **Purpose and expected transaction profile:** what is normal for this customer.
7. **Conclusions and gaps:** what could not be checked and how it affects risk.
8. **Approval** by management: the decision and conditions (limits, enhanced monitoring, review frequency).

### Monitoring after the decision

Enhanced observation of transactions, change indicators (changes of owners and directors, new sanctions, publications, gaps in reporting), re-screening on a schedule and on events, scheduled review of the file more often than for ordinary customers. The conditions and periods are recorded in the decision.

### When to refuse or exit

- a sanctions match is confirmed, or cannot be excluded;
- the beneficial owner or controller cannot be established;
- the customer refuses to provide the necessary information or provides forged documents;
- the source of funds is not confirmed and risk cannot be reduced by measures;
- signs of a scheme with no business purpose.

The procedure for refusal, reporting to the regulator and dealing with the customer is determined by law and the organisation's policy; the customer must not be warned of suspicions (section 1.2).

## 5.2 Typologies and red flags

A red flag is a reason for a deeper check, not a conclusion of wrongdoing; it becomes significant only in combination with independent indicators. The tables are generalised from general knowledge (G) and 2026 examples (S).

### Shell networks and complex ownership

| What is observed | Questions to check |
| --- | --- |
| Hundreds of companies at one address, one secretary | Are there staff, premises, a website, tax traces |
| Nominee directors with hundreds of positions | Who actually makes decisions |
| Multi-layer ownership in several countries | Where the chain breaks, who is at the end |
| Minimal financial statements or none | Is there a duty to publish |
| Registration shortly before a significant event | What it was created for, who created it |
| A stake just under a threshold (49%, 24.9%) | Splitting of control between related persons |

### Sanctions evasion

| What is observed | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| A new company with the same people, address, website after sanctions | Is it a successor |
| A transfer of shares close to the sanctions date | A break in control or its continuation |
| A change of manager, flag, vessel name; AIS gaps | Escaping risk, transhipment at sea |
| Transit through countries with weak oversight (in the EU's 20th package, Kyrgyzstan) | Re-export |
| Payments through new exchanges and payment services (Garantex → Grinex, A7A5) | Infrastructure successors |
| Networks rather than individuals (Prince Group, the Shamkhani network) | Who supplies, who ships, who receives payments |

### Financial statement manipulation

| What is observed | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Receivables grow faster than revenue | Who the buyers are, is there payment |
| Cash "held by third parties" without confirmation | Who confirms its existence |
| Revenue growth with unchanged headcount | Where the growth comes from |
| A high share of related parties | Who they are, on what terms |
| A change of auditor, qualifications | Why |

### Procurement and corruption risks

A new company wins large contracts; one winner on many similar tenders; a contract split below a threshold; links between the winner and officials or their relatives; a price above market without justification. Sources: procurement portals, registers, publications.

### Fake profiles and documents (KYC and hiring)

| What is observed | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| A generated photo, a fresh account, template texts | Provenance of the image, age and history of the account |
| The DPRK IT-worker scheme: stolen identities, fabricated personas, remote employment | Verify identity from an independent source, consistency of data, payment details (OFAC, 28 October 2026, S) |
| A diploma or licence not found in the register | Confirmation from the issuing body |
| IP, geolocation and claimed country do not match in remote onboarding | Additional verification, manual review |

### How not to get it wrong

1. A flag becomes a conclusion only in combination with independent indicators.
2. For every flag write the innocent explanation and the observation that would tell them apart.
3. Flags of the same kind do not add up: five indicators from one layer are still one layer.
4. Distinguish an "anomaly" (needs an explanation) from a "violation" (established by a court or regulator).
5. Record dates: a flag at the time of the event and now are different things.
